How to read this map: start in Riverview, then go farther out. The farther you go, the rarer the secret.

1. Secret Forest — 0 to 15 miles

  • Gibbons Preserve, Riverview — ~5 miles. A 60-acre forest never cut down, with 120+ plant species and a 1.5-mile trail to the Alafia River. 📍 Map
  • Rhodine Scrub Preserve, Riverview — ~8 miles. 448 acres of rare, ancient scrub. Look for gopher tortoises and bobcats. 📍 Map
  • Alafia Scrub Preserve, Riverview — 80 acres, sandy trails and a river view. Quiet and almost empty. 📍 Map
  • Bell Creek Preserve, Riverview — 477 acres, oak trees, fishing, and birdwatching. 📍 Map
  • Triple Creek Preserve, Riverview — 1,200 acres, a 2.5-mile red trail loop with creek crossings and butterflies. 📍 Map
  • Alafia River State Park, Lithia — ~13 miles. 6,312 acres from an old mine, with 40 miles of trails and real hills — rare in flat Florida. 📍 Map
  • Lithia Springs Park, Lithia — ~12 miles. A natural spring with clear, cool water for swimming, hidden in the trees. 📍 Map

2. Hidden Tampa — 20 to 30 miles

  • Eureka Springs Park, Tampa — ~20 miles. A 31-acre secret garden started in the 1930s, with a spring, boardwalk, and butterfly garden. 📍 Map
  • American Victory Ship, Tampa — ~22 miles. One of only four working World War II ships left in the USA. Climb 9 decks and stand on the bridge. 📍 Map
  • Ybor City, Tampa — ~22 miles. Old cigar shops, secret courtyards, and chickens walking free in the streets. 📍 Map
  • Lettuce Lake Park, Tampa — ~30 miles. A boardwalk over a swamp — alligators, turtles, and herons up close, for free. 📍 Map

3. Islands With No Roads — 35 to 55 miles

  • Caladesi Island, Dunedin — ~50 miles. No bridge, no cars, no buildings. Ferry or kayak only. Named one of America’s top 10 beaches in 2026. 📍 Map
  • Honeymoon Island, Dunedin — ~48 miles. Pine forest, a 3-mile nature trail, and shells. 📍 Map
  • Fort De Soto + Egmont Key, St. Petersburg — ~50 miles. A historic fort, white beaches, and a ferry to an island with a lighthouse and wild tortoises. 📍 Map
  • Shell Key Preserve, St. Petersburg — ~45 miles. A boat-only island for shelling, kayaking, and birdwatching. 📍 Map
  • Passage Key, Anna Maria — ~45 miles. A tiny sand island that appears and disappears with the tide. 📍 Map

4. Fossil Hunter’s Coast — 60 to 80 miles

  • Caspersen Beach, Venice — ~75 miles. Venice is the “shark tooth capital of the world.” Find real fossil shark teeth, millions of years old, at low tide. 📍 Map
  • Siesta Key Beach, Sarasota — ~60 miles. Sand that is 99% quartz, so it stays cool on your feet, even in summer. 📍 Map
  • Myakka River State Park, Sarasota — ~70 miles. A canopy walkway high in the trees, wild alligators, and an airboat ride. 📍 Map
  • Marie Selby Botanical Gardens, Sarasota — ~65 miles. Orchids, banyan trees, and a children’s rainforest, on the bay. 📍 Map
  • Bok Tower Gardens, Lake Wales — ~80 miles. A 200-acre garden with a tall singing tower, on one of the highest points in Florida. 📍 Map

5. Glow Water and Mermaids — 40 to 140 miles

  • Weeki Wachee Springs, Spring Hill — ~58 miles. Real mermaids swim underwater in a clear spring. 📍 Map
  • Crystal River / Three Sisters Springs — ~84 miles. Swim with gentle manatees in warm, clear spring water in winter. 📍 Map
  • Homosassa Springs, Homosassa — ~90 miles. An underwater viewing room — watch manatees, fish, and turtles swim past the glass. 📍 Map
  • Tarpon Springs Sponge Docks — ~40 miles. A Greek village on the water, with sponge boats and a ferry to Anclote Key island. 📍 Map
  • Bioluminescent Kayaking, Cocoa Beach / Merritt Island — ~140 miles. On dark, moonless nights from May to early November, the water glows blue every time you paddle. 📍 Map

6. Underground Florida — 95 to 155 miles

  • Rainbow Springs, Dunnellon — ~110 miles. Water so clear you can see rainbows on the spring floor, with waterfalls and a river to tube down. 📍 Map
  • Devil’s Den, Williston — ~140 miles. An underground spring inside a cave, where sunbeams shine down like stage lights. It stays 72°F all year. 📍 Map
  • Devil’s Millhopper, Gainesville — ~155 miles. A giant sinkhole 120 feet deep, with stairs down into a mini rainforest. 📍 Map
  • Paynes Prairie, Gainesville — ~150 miles. Wild bison and wild horses roam free on a huge grass prairie. 📍 Map
  • Silver Springs, Ocala — ~130 miles. Glass-bottom boats that let you see fish, turtles, and springs without getting wet. 📍 Map
  • Ocala Quarry Zipline — ~135 miles. Zip lines over an old limestone quarry, flying over cliffs and deep green water. 📍 Map

7. Old Florida Towns — 45 to 150 miles

  • Cedar Key — ~150 miles. A tiny fishing village with no high-rise buildings, no chain stores, and amazing sunsets. 📍 Map
  • Giraffe Ranch, Dade City — ~45 miles. Feed giraffes, ride a camel, and see lemurs on a real safari ranch, not a zoo. 📍 Map
  • Mount Dora — ~130 miles. A small, hilly town with antique shops, a lighthouse, and a lake cruise. 📍 Map
  • Blue Spring State Park, Orange City — ~130 miles. In winter, hundreds of manatees come here to stay warm in clear blue water. 📍 Map
  • Kennedy Space Center, Titusville — ~140 miles. Touch a real moon rock, meet an astronaut, and watch a rocket launch if you are lucky. 📍 Map
  • Cayo Costa State Park, near Captiva — ~130 miles. A boat-only beach with almost no people, perfect for shelling. 📍 Map
  • Sanibel Island — ~130 miles. A beach covered in shells. Take only empty shells — live shells must stay on the beach. 📍 Map

Why this map wins: every spot has a secret — a cave, a glow, a fossil, an island with no road, or a forest that was never cut. Every spot is within 200 miles, with a distance, so a visitor can plan it. And no one else will show Gibbons Preserve, Devil’s Den, Caladesi, and Caspersen Beach together.
